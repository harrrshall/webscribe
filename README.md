### **Part 3: Unit IV Exam Cheatsheet**
**1. Key Definitions**
* **Wear:** Undesirable removal of material from operating solid surfaces.
* **Scuffing:** A form of adhesive wear causing rough marks/welding due to lubricant failure.
* **Galling:** Severe adhesive wear where material is torn from one surface and stuck to the other.
* **Fretting:** Wear caused by small amplitude oscillatory motion (vibration).
**2. Archard’s Wear Equation (The "Golden Formula")**
$$V = \frac{K \cdot W \cdot L}{H}$$
* $V$: Wear Volume ($m^3$)
* $K$: Wear Coefficient (dimensionless, probability of debris formation)
* $W$: Normal Load ($N$)
* $L$: Sliding Distance ($m$)
* $H$: Hardness of the softer material ($Pa$ or $N/m^2$).
### **3. Mechanisms Summary**
| **Mechanism**      | **Cause**                                                     | **Appearance**                       | **Prevention**                                              |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Adhesive Wear**  | Micro-welding of asperities during sliding                    | Scuffing, tearing, material transfer | Increase hardness, use compatible metals, apply lubrication |
| **Abrasive Wear**  | Hard asperities or foreign particles cutting a softer surface | Grooves, scratches, cutting marks    | Filter oil to remove particles, harden the surface          |
| **Fatigue Wear**   | Repeated cyclic loading/unloading                             | Pitting, surface cracks, spalling    | Reduce load, improve surface finish                         |
| **Corrosive Wear** | Chemical reactions combined with sliding motion               | Pits, rusted debris, oxide formation | Use corrosion-resistant materials, apply inhibitors         |
**4. Delamination Theory Steps (Suh)**
1. Plastic deformation of surface.
2. Subsurface void/crack nucleation (at dislocations).
3. Crack propagation parallel to surface.
4. Shear/fracture to surface creating sheet-like debris.
**5. Wear Testing Method**
* **Pin-on-Disc:** Standard test where a stationary pin is loaded against a rotating disc to measure wear volume and friction coefficient.
## **Q1. Hydrodynamic vs Hydrostatic vs Boundary Lubrication + Stribeck Curve**
**Hydrodynamic:**
• Film by **relative motion (wedge action)**
• **No metal contact**, very low μ
**Hydrostatic:**
• **Externally pumped high-pressure** fluid
• No relative motion needed
**Boundary:**
• **Thin/molecular film**, asperity contact
• High μ; at start/stop & high loads
**Stribeck Curve:**
Plot **μ vs (ZN/P)**. Shows transition:
**Boundary → Mixed → Hydrodynamic**.
## **Q2. Assumptions in Reynolds' Equation**
• **Newtonian**, **incompressible** lubricant
• **Laminar** flow
• **Thin film** (h ≪ bearing radius)
• **No-slip** at boundaries
• **Negligible inertia** (viscous forces dominate)
• Pressure **constant across film thickness** (varies only along length/width)
## **Q3. Surface Engineering: Treatments vs Coatings**
**Surface Engineering:** Change **surface properties** (hardness, wear, corrosion) without altering bulk.
**Surface Treatments (Modification):**
• Alter microstructure **within substrate**
• Examples: **Carburizing, Nitriding, Induction Hardening**
**Surface Coatings (Deposition):**
• **New material layer** added on top
• Examples: **Electroplating, PVD, CVD**
## **Q4. Lubricant Additives (3 Types + Functions)**

• **Anti-wear (AW):** Protective film under load (e.g., **ZDDP**)
• **Viscosity Index Improvers:** Maintain viscosity at high T
• **Antioxidants:** Prevent oxidation, sludge, acid formation
## **Q5. Viscosity Index (VI) & Flash Point**
**Viscosity Index (VI):**
High VI → small viscosity change with T
Low VI → large change with T
**Flash Point:**
Lowest T where oil gives vapor that **ignites momentarily**. Safety indicator.
